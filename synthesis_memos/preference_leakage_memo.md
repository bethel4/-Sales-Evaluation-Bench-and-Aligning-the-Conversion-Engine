# synthesis_memos/preference_leakage_memo.md

## Paper

Preference Leakage: A Contamination Problem in LLM-as-a-Judge
(Li et al., 2025)

---

## Design Choice

The paper argues that **using the same model for both generation and evaluation introduces bias**, and recommends separating generator and judge models to prevent preference leakage.

---

## Disagreement

I agree with the core argument, but I disagree with the implication that **model separation alone is sufficient to prevent leakage**.

---

## Evidence from My Project

In my dataset construction pipeline:

* Model A (Qwen) generates “chosen” outputs
* Model B (DeepSeek) evaluates outputs

This follows the paper’s recommendation.

However, I observed that:

* Even with model separation, generated outputs often share similar structure and phrasing
* Judge decisions can still be biased toward certain stylistic patterns
* Some low-quality tasks passed initial filtering due to weak rubric clarity

This shows that leakage is not only:

* model-level

but also:

* **data-distribution-level**

---

## Why This Matters

The paper focuses on **model identity**, but in practice:

> leakage can occur through shared patterns in generated data

For example:

* synthetic tasks generated by one model may still bias another model
* if prompts are not diverse or grounded
* the judge may learn superficial signals instead of real correctness

---

## My Adjustment

To address this, I extended beyond model separation:

1. **Multi-source data**

   * trace-derived (real failures)
   * programmatic templates
   * multi-LLM synthesis

2. **Judge filtering**

   * scoring on coherence, verifiability, rubric clarity

3. **Pairwise comparison**

   * removing near-duplicate tasks

This reduces:

* pattern repetition
* superficial bias

---

## Conclusion

I agree that preference leakage is a critical issue, but I argue that:

> model separation is necessary but not sufficient

Effective mitigation requires:

* diverse data sources
* strong filtering
* and grounding in real failures

In my pipeline, leakage prevention is achieved through a **combination of model rotation and dataset design**, not model rotation alone.
